# RealFlag

Cloth flags that wave in the wind, for **Cities: Skylines** (the first game). Every flag is a
real piece of cloth, simulated as it hangs and flutters, and drawn with a fabric shader in the
game's own lighting: the sun and its shadows, the street lights at night, the fog.

A flag comes **without a pole** unless you ask for one. Its reference point is the top corner of
its hoist edge, where a pole's halyard would clip on, and the hoist edge hangs down from it --
straight down, or along a tilted pole; or, for a banner hoisted from its top, runs across from
it. Put it on a pole of your choosing, a mast, a roof or a facade; or let the mod draw a
flagpole under it.

## Using it

Open the panel with **F6** (or its Unified UI button). It opens on the city's **Flags**. Its
look is the options page's **Panel look**: Frosted glass (the default, over a blurred copy of the
city), See-through, Blue, or Vanilla, the game's own.

**+ New flag** (at the top of the Flags tab) opens the new flag's window beside the panel, and
the flag follows the pointer, blowing, standing on whatever is under it (ground, roof, road;
pointed at a wall, the ground in front of it): with a pole, the pole's foot is there and the flag
at its top; without one, the flag's hoist. Click to place it, and again for more. The right
button turns it as the game's props turn: a right click turns it 45 degrees, a drag with the
right button turns it freely (it holds its place meanwhile). **Page Up** and **Page Down** (the
game's raise and lower keys) raise and lower the whole flag, pole and all, off what it stands
on, as Move It raises and lowers what it moves: a step of 12.5 cm a press, on their own once held
a third of a second, Shift eight times faster, Alt eight times finer -- the way to put a flag
without a pole where it hangs. The pointer's tip says how far, and the flags you place keep it,
until you close the window. Close the window, or press Esc, when done. In the
window: a country's flag -- every country and territory, England, Scotland, Wales, Northern
Ireland, the EU and the UN, flown as it is at its own proportions (search by name or code) -- or a pattern
(plain, bands, crosses, a saltire, a canton, stripes and canton, a disc, a hoist triangle, a
diagonal band, a border) in up to three colours (a palette, hue, saturation and brightness, or
a hex code typed, pasted or copied); its **shape** as it hangs, from a tall banner to a long
flag, picked from little pictures of each; its **width** and **height** (0.5 to 30 m each,
changing together unless you switch that off, from 1:5 to 5:1); the edge it is **hoisted
from** -- its side, as flags fly, or its top, as a banner hangs from a crossbar (its design then
turns as a flag hung upright does: the canton at the top on the left); its **motion** -- it
**waves** in the wind, keeps **still**, or is held **flat**; how wavy the cloth is, how dark
(for a flag whose white or yellow glares in a bright sun: its colours as if dyed darker, up to
80%), and the way it points.

**Motion**: a **still** flag keeps one shape for good, caught as it flew in a steady breeze
blowing the way it points (turn it to aim its fly; a banner hoisted from its top hangs), with
How wavy setting how rippled; flags of the same design share the same shape. A **flat** flag
is held flat, as on a wall or in a banner's frame. Neither moves with the wind or the weather,
and once made they cost nothing to run -- for rows of flags, flags indoors or on walls, or a
city whose flags should hold a pose. Switched back to waving, a flag flies on from where it was.

**Your image** (in the new flag's window and in the Flags tab): a picture of your own on the
flag. The folder button beside it opens the images folder, `RealFlag\Images` in the game's
data folder next to `RealFlag.xml` (on Windows
`%LOCALAPPDATA%\Colossal Order\Cities_Skylines\RealFlag\Images`); put `.png` or `.jpg` pictures
in it. The name button opens the **Image** window: your pictures in a list, each with a
thumbnail -- new ones appear by themselves while it is open -- and, under it, the flag as it
hangs with the picture on it and what is cut off dimmed round it. Picking a picture gives the
flag its shape, so all of it shows; the picture is **never stretched**. While **width and height
go together**, the flag keeps the picture's shape as you size it (switched back on, it takes it
back); switch that off to give the flag any shape, the picture cropped to it. Drag it to move it on
the flag, use the wheel or the Zoom slider to zoom (100%: it just fills the flag; less shows all
of it with the flag's own pattern round it), **Fill**, **Whole picture**, **Turn 90°**, and
**Flag takes its shape** again after a change. A picture's see-through parts (a PNG's
transparency) are **cut out** -- a pennant or swallowtail PNG makes a shaped flag -- or **show
the flag** through them, its own pattern and colours: a logo on a coloured flag. On a banner
hoisted from its top a picture stays upright. Pictures over 2048 pixels are scaled down. A
flag keeps its picture by file name: a city opened where the file is missing shows the flag's
pattern until the file is back, and an edited picture shows on its flags when the Image window
is next open.

**Flagpole** (a button in the new flag's window and in the Flags tab, which opens beside them): none by default -- the flag
is for a pole of your own -- or a pole the mod draws, its **Pole height** from its foot to the
flag: 64-sided, tapered like a real one and as thick as one for that flag and height, with
a flash collar at its foot, a truck at its top and a gilt ball, a flat cap or a spear on it,
one or two halyard lines, your colours for the pole, its top and its ropes, and a thickness to
taste. With ropes, the flag hangs from its top and bottom clips only, as a hoisted flag does,
and its edge bows out between them in the wind; without, it is held along its whole edge. The
cloth keeps off the pole: a flag the wind turns back over its pole wraps round it. A banner
hoisted from its top gets a crossbar instead, reaching to a wall it hangs near. The pole is
part of the flag: raised or lowered with Move It or Page Up and Page Down, the flag takes its
pole with it. A selected flag's **Pole height** makes its pole taller or shorter, its foot where
it stands and the flag going with the top, and **Pole down to the ground** (the same window)
sends a moved pole down to whatever stands under it again. Here
too the pole's **tilt** and **lean** (a wall-mounted pole leans 30 to 45 degrees, a horizontal
one 90) -- the flag hangs from the tilted line whether a pole is drawn or not.

**Flags**: every flag in the city. Click one in the list (or **Pick on the map**) to
select it: its design, size, pole and rotation are in the tab, and it carries a
**gizmo** -- drag its red, green and blue arrows to move it along its own axes (the white knob
slides it anywhere at its height), its rings to turn it, tilt it or lean it; hold Ctrl to snap,
Shift to go finely. **Page Up** and **Page Down** raise and lower it, pole and all, as they do
while placing. Click elsewhere on the map, right-click or press Esc to let it go (Esc during
a drag puts the flag back). **Go to** flies the camera there, **Delete** removes it, **New flag
like this** opens the new flag's window with its design, to place another.

**Settings**: the **wind** is the game's own by default -- its direction, which turns slowly
over time; a gentle breeze made stronger by rain and calmer by fog, as the game's weather has
it; more wind on hills and less in valleys and by forests, as on the game's wind map; and more
the higher a flag flies. Or switch it off and set your own: the speed at 10 m (the panel shows
its Beaufort force), how gusty it is, and where it blows -- along each flag (every flag flies
the way it was placed), one fixed direction for all, or the game's direction at your speed.
Here too the **cloth detail**, whether the cloth **collides with itself** and whether it **keeps
off buildings, props and trees**, and whether flags stop while the game is paused.

**While the game is paused** the flags stand still (an option), but a flag you move, turn or
redesign comes to life under your hand and settles back into a still a moment after.

### With Move It

Flags are the game's props, so **Move It** selects, moves, turns, raises, copies, mirrors and
deletes them like any other, with undo. A copied flag keeps its design, and a flag raised or
lowered keeps its pole's length: the whole flag moves, pole and all. However fast it is moved --
dragged, raised with Page Up, flung across the map -- its cloth goes with it, leaning a little
into the move as a real one would, never through itself; lowered into the ground, it hangs into
the ground from its hoist rather than being torn from it. Move It's highlight is a
steady disc round everywhere the flag can reach, and its pole. Move It picks the flag by where
its cloth has flown over the last few seconds and a margin round it, its hoist and its pole: a
target that holds still while the cloth flutters and is never thinner than a fingertip on the
screen, however far off, edge on or high it flies, without reaching far over what stands round
it (the game's own tools, the bulldozer among them, keep to the cloth itself, its hoist and its
pole). A pole asset right beside a flag's hoist can be behind that target: Move It's step-over
or its filters pick it. This needs the Harmony mod, which the game offers to subscribe to when
it is missing.

## Requirements and compatibility

- **Harmony** (Workshop 2040656402) is required; the game offers to subscribe to it when it is
  missing. **Move It** and **Unified UI** are optional.
- **Windows** is where the mod is made and tested. The **Mac** (Metal) and **Linux** (OpenGL)
  have shaders of their own, checked in the editor but not yet run on either: new and
  experimental. On a Mac the panel's key is Fn+F6 on most keyboards (the button always works),
  and Page Up / Page Down are Fn+Up / Fn+Down on a laptop.
- The log (`RealFlag.log`), the settings (`RealFlag.xml`) and your images folder
  (`RealFlag/Images`) are in the game's data folder: `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Colossal Order\Cities_Skylines`
  on Windows, `~/Library/Application Support/Colossal Order/Cities_Skylines` on a Mac,
  `~/.local/share/Colossal Order/Cities_Skylines` on Linux.
- **Extended Managers Library** (which Prop Anarchy's larger prop limit uses) keeps a city's
  props where RealFlag cannot find them: with it enabled, RealFlag does nothing in a city and
  says so, and saving the city keeps its flags' designs exactly as they were.
- **Other tools that place props** (Find It, Picker, the game's own prop tool) can place
  RealFlag's prop, which then becomes a flag with the new flag's design. Place flags with
  RealFlag's own tool, or copy them with Move It, which keeps their designs.
- **Procedural Objects** can't turn a flag into one (its own Convert to PO or Move It's): it
  would copy only a flat placeholder, not the cloth. RealFlag refuses and the flag stays a flag;
  in a selection, Move It converts the rest.
- Only one copy of RealFlag may be enabled: a copy in the game's local Mods folder and a
  subscribed one would both run.

## Saves

The flags are the game's own props, and their designs are saved with the city under a key of
the mod's own. Without the mod a city loads without its flags, as a city does without any prop
asset it is missing: nothing else in it changes, and if it is not saved, its flags are all back
with the mod (a save made without the mod has lost them). A city whose flags this copy of the
mod cannot read (saved by a newer version) or cannot see (Extended Managers Library) is left
alone, and its flags' designs are saved back exactly as they were loaded; a city that loses its
flags while it loads -- which the mod has never seen happen -- says so, and asks not to be saved.

## How it works

- **The cloth** is a grid of particles held together by distance constraints: position-based
  dynamics (Jakobsen 2001; Müller et al. 2007), with long range attachments to the hoist so it
  never stretches like rubber (Kim, Chentanez and Müller 2012) and shorter steps in strong
  wind (Macklin et al. 2019). The hoist edge is pinned where the pole would be.
- **It does not go through itself**: every particle is a sphere as wide as the grid's spacing
  that no other may enter (Macklin et al. 2014; Müller's *Ten Minute Physics*) -- wider at a finer
  detail, whose particles move as far in a step through smaller cells -- found through a
  spatial hash (Teschner et al. 2003) and a neighbour list (Verlet 1967), and skipped while the
  cloth is too flat to fold onto itself (Volino and Magnenat-Thalmann 1994). A flag hanging in
  calm air lays its folds on one another. A new flag is let down into the air, damped as a
  pendulum of its length is critically damped, rather than dropped flat to whip through itself --
  the dynamic relaxation that brings a laid-out membrane to rest (Day 1965; Barnes 1988) -- and a
  moved flag's cloth is dragged through the air no faster than it can follow, the rest of the move
  carrying it. A cloth that ever knots all the same (its worst link 30% long for two seconds) is
  laid out afresh.
- **It keeps off the city**: the top of whatever stands round a flag -- the ground, the water,
  the roofs, from the buildings' own low-detail meshes (the triangles the game picks a building
  by) -- is laid on a fine grid round it, and the cloth stays above it, as the position-based
  collision constraints of Müller et al. 2007 keep it: a flag hangs onto a roof, a banner lies
  along the facade it hangs by, a flag blown at a wall stops at it. A wall is where the grid
  climbs to its roof: the cloth stops a few centimetres short of it rather than into it. Props
  and trees are the upright capsules the game itself picks them by, which the cloth keeps off as
  it keeps off its pole; one round the hoist (a pole of the city's own under a flag) is narrowed
  to leave the hoist outside; a wall a pole stands against is kept back from its hoist. A flag
  dragged through or along a building (Move It, the placing pointer) is not snapped out of it: what
  the building sweeps into is let out no faster than a dragged flag moves -- the cap NVIDIA PhysX
  puts on an overlap's way out (its maximum depenetration velocity). A new flag is laid out the
  first way clear of the walls round it.
  A flag high above everything costs nothing more. Not yet: roads and bridges; anything over a
  flag (an arcade, a canopy -- a flag whose hoist stands under a roof leaves that roof out);
  still and flat flags, which keep the shape they were made with.
- **Cloth detail** (the options page, or the foot of the panel): Low, Medium, High (the
  default), Very high, Ultra or Extreme -- 18 to 64 cells along a flag. Only flags big on the
  screen get the finest; the others as fine as their size there needs. Ultra and Extreme refine
  flags over about 3 m (smaller ones are at their finest already) and cost two to four times
  Very high's.
- **The drawn surface** is smooth: a flag big on the screen is drawn up to four times finer
  than its particles, on the Catmull-Rom surface through them (Catmull and Rom 1974), worked
  out on the GPU from the particles in a small texture, with its normals from the surface; its
  edges are smoothed as curves first (Taubin 1995), so a flying flag's outline and folds are
  curves, not polygons -- and the physics costs what it did.
- **The pole** is sized as flagpoles are, for the wind's bending moment on its flag at its
  height (its foot grows as the cube root of flag area times height), and is a cylinder the
  cloth's particles keep off. A tilted hoist turns the whole cloth with it (Rodrigues' rotation).
  On its halyard a flag is held at its two clips, the lower drawn up 3% of the hoist (a halyard
  is never bar-tight), and its heading -- stiffer than the flag, as canvas is -- is free between.
- **The air** acts on it as the theory of the fluttering flag has it: the added mass of the air
  the cloth carries sideways, pushed along by the flow (Lighthill 1960; Eloy, Souilliez and
  Schouveiler 2007), which is what makes a flag in a steady wind flutter by itself, and a flat
  plate's drag across the cloth (Hoerner 1965), which limits how far. Along the cloth, only
  the air's skin friction (Prandtl's law): a flag streams out on the drag of its own waves, and
  big flags fold and flap in a strong wind. Waves run from the hoist to the fly, slower than
  the wind, and grow towards the fly, as real flags' do. The less wavy a flag is set, the
  stiffer and rougher its cloth.
- **The wind** follows the Eurocode for towns (EN 1991-1-4): more wind higher up, gusts of the
  right size and strength, and eddies the size of a flag carried past it with the wind, so
  flags a few metres apart feel the same gust one after the other. The game's own wind, which
  has a direction but no speed, gives it its direction, and its weather and wind map (the ones
  its wind turbines use) make a gentle breeze stronger or calmer.
- **The shader** is a two-sided fabric: the "Charlie" sheen of cloth at grazing angles
  (Estevez and Kulla 2017; Neubelt and Pettineo 2013), light shining through the cloth from
  behind (GPU Gems ch. 16), and the design drawn from its colours -- or a country's flag from
  its image, cut out where the image is (Nepal's two pennants), or your picture where you laid
  it -- with a canvas heading, two grommets and hems. Dry, it is matte; in the rain it gets wet
  as the city's ground does (darker, with the water's glints on its folds), and dries with it.
- **A still flag** is flown once, in the flag's own frame, a few steps a frame: laid out flat,
  then a few seconds in a steady breeze -- Beaufort force 3's, "light flags extended", for a
  flag up to 3 m, more for a bigger one by Froude's scaling (the same U²/gL: a big flag holds
  out against its weight as a small one does). Over its last two seconds the shape is weighed
  every eighth of a second and the smoothest kept that shows the most of the design (the
  grid's squared second differences, a thin-plate bending energy, over the area facing out):
  a moment taken blindly often froze the fly's corner mid-flick. The same design gives the same
  shape every time, turned to where each flag points; it is never stepped again.
- **Your pictures** are cropped, never stretched: the flag shows a window of the picture with
  the flag's own shape, as large as it goes at 100%. A picture's hidden colours under its
  see-through pixels are filled from its visible ones (the "pull-push" of Gortler et al.'s
  Lumigraph, 1996), so its cut or blended edges show no dark fringe.
- The cloth is stepped on threads of its own (one per six of the machine's, up to four), 60
  times a second, while the game draws the frame; the flags biggest on the screen first, within
  a time budget that grows with the frame's length, and flags that had to wait go first next
  time. How far a flag is drawn and moves goes by its size on the screen, not a fixed distance:
  a zoomed camera keeps a far flag it shows large (a 3 m flag with the game's usual camera is
  drawn to about 4 km and waves to about 1.3 km; a 20 m flag several times as far).

## Building

See [BUILDING.md](BUILDING.md). In short: `.\build-bundle.ps1` compiles the cloth shader with
Unity 5.6.6 for Windows, the Mac and Linux (`-Only win` for Windows alone, while working on it),
then `dotnet build RealFlag/RealFlag.csproj` builds the mod and deploys it to the game's Mods
folder.
